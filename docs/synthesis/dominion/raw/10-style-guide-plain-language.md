- "These are the rules to create web content that can be easily found, understood and used. They are based on writing principles and techniques that help make web content clear and adapted to the needs of all people. Use it with the Canada.ca Specifications when you're designing and organizing web content."
- Mandatory: "All departments and other portions of the federal public administration subject to the Directive on the Management of Communications must use the Canada.ca Style Guide... for all public-facing websites and digital services, regardless of the technology, domain name or publishing platform used." Referenced in Appendix D: Mandatory Procedures for Social Media and Web Communications.
- 1.0 Writing principles: reflect how people read and use web content.
- 1.1 Help people complete tasks: transactional or informational; "People trying to complete a task online usually don't read word by word. They scan quickly for words and links."
- GoC web content must be: intuitive; comprehensive; targeted (most important information first, tailored to audience); consistent ("use a standardized approach so that people can scan content quickly and easily with a sense of confidence and trust").
- 1.2 Consider the needs of the audience: accessible, clear, adapted to widest audience.
  - Writing for accessibility: wide range of people incl. physical/cognitive disabilities; consider assistive technologies; refer to WCAG 2.0.
  - Writing for inclusivity: "Make gender-inclusive writing your standard practice."
  - Writing for readability: ease of reading/understanding; consider audience's reading level and literacy needs.
- Also references Writing Tips Plus; guide takes priority for online content when conflicts arise.
