# Treatment of the official languages

Heads of communications are responsible for ensuring that Canada's official languages are represented equally in the department's corporate identity.

## Visual equality
In a department's corporate identity, the 2 official languages must be treated with equal prominence, including their use of the same colours, style, size and type.

## Order of the official languages in signatures
Must appear in a side-by-side, bilingual form in all corporate and ministerial signatures. Order determined by factors such as location, language of content, and distribution; if several conflicting requirements, determined by the department's head of communications.

English to the left of French when:
- content of the item is English only
- content is in a bilingual side-by-side format with English to the left of French
- office issuing the item is located outside the province of Quebec
- signature identifies the English portion of an item produced in a bilingual tumble format
- item is intended for national or government-wide use and distribution
- item is physically located (signage) or operates (motor vehicles) outside of the province of Quebec
- content is in a language other than English or French

French to the left of English when:
- content of the item is French only
- content is in a bilingual side-by-side format with French to the left of English
- office issuing the item is located in the province of Quebec
- signature identifies the French portion of an item produced in a bilingual tumble format
- content is of particular importance to the Francophone community (for example, Jeux de la Francophonie)
- item is physically located (signage) or operates (motor vehicles) in the province of Quebec

National Capital Region: for motor vehicles and department-issued clothing used in the NCR, the order of official languages on opposing vehicle doors and on uniform shoulder flashes may be alternated.

## Formats
Messaging in a product must be presented in one of 2 ways:
- bilingual format: side-by-side (languages next to each other) or over-and-under format (one above the other)
- separate versions in each language

Side-by-side format required for: business cards (individuals may use a side-by-side or double-sided format); primary identification signs; directory boards; common-use and operational signs; project signs; commemorative plaques; markings that identify motor vehicles, aircraft and watercraft; personnel identification.

Where horizontal space is limited, an over-and-under format is permitted for: common-use and operational signs; personnel identification. For over-and-under, the language on top is the one that would appear to the left in a side-by-side format.

Separate versions: departments can choose between a double-sided format and separate unilingual versions for communications products and ads, except out-of-home ads, which must use a bilingual format. Where separate unilingual versions are produced, content must state that the same material is available in the other official language. Phrasing such as version francaise disponible to be used where applicable (brochures, forms, publications).

## Use of other languages
The Canada wordmark and titles used in signatures must not be translated into another language. They must appear in English and French only. Additional languages can be added to messaging on products; they must appear either to the right of or below the English and French. Messaging in all languages must be visually equal.
