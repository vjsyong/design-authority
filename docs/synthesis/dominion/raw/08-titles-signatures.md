Title: Titles and signatures: Design Standard for the Federal Identity Program - Canada.ca
Fetched: 2026-10-07 (hound, ok; 2 pages: main + tail)

KEY CONTENT (abridged but verbatim on rules):
- 2 types of titles: applied titles, ministerial titles.
- Applied titles: department's official name used in all communications with the public and in its corporate signature; mandatory under the Policy on Communications and Federal Identity. Not legal titles (legal titles only where required by law).
- Criteria: must include the word "Canada" (jurisdiction, not "Canadian" = nationality); EN and FR equivalent respecting language style/grammar; as short as possible; keywords related to core function only; no acronyms, abbreviations or special characters (e.g. ampersand). Applied titles should be no more than 2 to 4 words long, excluding articles.
- Abbreviation criteria: different from registered/other major orgs; letters representing keywords of EN+FR title (e.g. HC, CanNor); max 6 letters, no special chars; no articles/conjunctions; EN/FR equivalent.
- Examples: Government of Canada (GC) / Gouvernement du Canada (GC); Health Canada (HC) / Sante Canada (SC); Parks Canada (PC) / Parcs Canada (PC); Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) / Ressources naturelles Canada (RNCan).
- Approval: minister + President of the Treasury Board before use; Registry of Applied Titles; TBS Communications and Federal Identity Policy Centre creates digital master files of the departmental signature.
- Ministerial titles = styled titles given at swearing in, used with arms of Canada; "Office of" / "Cabinet du" added for staff products; parliamentary secretaries add designation.
- Corporate signature = flag symbol or arms of Canada + EN and FR applied title.
- Flag signatures: flag symbol left + EN/FR applied title right, "set in Helvetica light, regular or medium weight". Most are 2 lines; 3-line signatures generally used for long titles. Must use digital master files (created/maintained by TBS).
- Government of Canada signature: flag symbol + "Government of Canada" in both official languages; used for government-wide/international items, multi-department activities/facilities, departments without an approved applied title.
- Arms signatures: arms of Canada + EN/FR applied title, "set in Helvetica light, regular or medium weight". Most 2 or 3 lines; 4-line for long titles. Two types: asymmetrical (arms left, title right; used in all products unless otherwise specified) and symmetrical (arms centre, one language each side; e.g. decision letters from federal tribunals).
- Signatures with service titles: branch/unit/program name below corporate signature, left-aligned with applied title; begin with a keyword, as short as possible, avoid repeating keywords from applied title, no acronyms/abbreviations/special characters. Applied title and service title: same typeface and size; separated by a 0.5 to 1-line space; may use contrasting weights of Helvetica (e.g. Helvetica light with Helvetica medium).
- Ministerial signatures: arms of Canada centred between EN and FR title of the minister, "set in Helvetica light, regular or medium weight".
- Musical signature: first 4 notes of "O Canada", lasts 1.5 seconds; in video products plays while the wordmark is on screen; standard version available; departments may create alternative instrumental variations.
- Copies of signatures via FIP coordinator.
