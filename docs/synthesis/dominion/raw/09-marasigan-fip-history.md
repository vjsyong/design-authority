Title: Canadian Federal Identity Program - Marasigan Design (reproducing Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat history text; published 2013-09-30)
Fetched: 2026-10-07 (hound, ok)

Background: FIP based on corporate symbols applied with organizational titles; corporate signatures identify institutions as part of GoC rather than separate entities.
- 1921: Canadian Coat of Arms adopted by royal proclamation, introduced on govt stationery. Red and white declared Canada's official colours.
- 1965: Canadian flag adopted; maple leaf confirmed as official national symbol.
- 1969: Official Languages Act proclaimed (equality of English and French).
- 1969: Task Force on Government Information report "To know and be known": government failing to make its presence known; organizations did not project a uniform, clearly identifiable image; many titles failed to distinguish public/private or federal/provincial; different uncoordinated symbols, many of poor design.
- 1970: creation of the Federal Identity Program announced in the House of Commons; standardization and clear identification of federal activities as main objectives; Information Canada made responsible.
- 1974: Treasury Board approved first policy guidelines incl. use of the two official languages and a management system.
- 1976: after Information Canada's demise, Treasury Board Secretariat given responsibility for FIP.
- 1978: Treasury Board issued a comprehensive policy and design standards.
- 1980: the "Canada" wordmark established as the global identifier of the government. 1987: the federal emblem (bar and maple leaf) was replaced by the Canadian flag. Coat of Arms, flag, and "Canada" wordmark are now the corporate symbols.
- Rationale (1981 Parliamentary Task Force quote): visibility/accountability — federal programs, services and contributions should be recognizable to the public in order for the government to be accountable.
- "The program's guidelines are based on the use of plain, non-bureaucratic language, functional graphic design and a systems approach in identifying government services."
- Scope: one of the largest corporate identity programs by a national government; est. 18,000 facilities, 16,000 government vehicles + forms, stationery, published material, advertisements; applied by over 100 federal institutions in all regions and abroad.
- "l'image Canada" concept (1969 Task Force): abroad, the distinction between government and Canada merges into a single image. 1988 Centre Pompidou Paris exhibition "Images d'utilite publique" included FIP as a case study.
