# Official symbols

The Government of Canada uses 2 official symbols to communicate its identity, the Canada wordmark and a corporate signature.

## Canada wordmark
The Canada wordmark is the global identifier of the Government of Canada. It appears with a corporate signature, in all fields of application, unless otherwise stipulated.

### Canada wordmark design
Composed of:
- the word "Canada" in a graphically modified version of the Baskerville typeface
- the flag symbol
The Canada wordmark can only be reproduced using the digital master file.

### Animated Canada wordmark
May be used in certain government communications products, for example, videos. When used, the animated wordmark must always appear in its static form as the final image and must be either:
- in black type with the FIP red flag symbol on a white background OR
- in white type with the FIP red flag symbol on a black background (recommended for large scale formats, such as television and cinema advertising)

## Corporate signatures
A corporate signature is made up of an English and French applied title and the flag symbol or the arms of Canada.

- Corporate signatures using the flag symbol identify departments, agencies, commissions, boards, councils, and other federal bodies that fall under Schedules I, I.1 and II of the Financial Administration Act, unless authorized to be identified by the arms of Canada.
- Corporate signatures using the arms of Canada identify: ministers and their offices; parliamentary secretaries; ambassadors; heads of consulates or missions; Clerk of the Privy Council; departments whose heads report directly to Parliament; departments that have a quasi-judicial function.

## Legal protection
Official symbols legally protected under: Trademarks Act s.9(1) (Prohibited marks); Copyright Act; Article 6ter of the Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property.

## Using other identifiers and symbols
- Additional corporate identifiers: Treasury Board approval required, sought through a Treasury Board submission.
- Specialized symbols: departments may use certain specialized symbols if criteria in the Policy on Communications and Federal Identity are met and approved by heads of communications.
- Non-GoC logos: when products are part of a formal partnering/sponsorship arrangement with external entities, the logo of each organization can be displayed (per terms of the agreement).
- Icons of social media providers: can be used when linking to an official social media account or when promoting it.
